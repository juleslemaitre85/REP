# REP
Reproductibilty of experimentations

You can reproduce the experiences by running the following programms:
  - egal.c for the associativity experience, to see if (x+y)+z = x+(y+z)
  - banking.c for the banking problem
The results will be printed respectively in the answers_associativity.txt and answers_banking.txt files.
The previous results are already printed in those.

The factors that could have an effect on the computed answer are the programming language, the number of repetitions, the system running the program, and the program itself, how it was written. There is also the mathematical property that is being tested: associativity or distributivity.

Two programs were generated by ai, banking_ai.py and associativity_ai.py, using claude.ai.
The prompts are:
  Donne moi un programme en python qui vérifie si (x+y)+z=x+(y+z).  A chaque fois que le programme tourne, il vérifie 10000 fois pour des x, y et z différents (aléatoires) et écris dans le fichier answers_associativity_llm.txt le résultat sous cette forme: compteur_faux: 760915 
compteur_vrai: 9239085 
pourcentage de vrai: 92.390850  (attention le résultat doit être écrit à la suite, sans écraser les réponse précédentes).
and: 
  Ecris un programme en python; The banking problem: do the same. "My banker proposed this investment to me: you give me e ≈ 2.71828… The following year, I take 1 euro as a fee and multiply by 1. The next year, I take 1 euro as a fee and multiply by 2. The next year, I take 1 euro as a fee and multiply by 3. […] After n years, I take 1 euro as a fee and multiply by n. To retrieve my money, there is a 1 euro fee. In 50 years, for my retirement, how much money will I have?" Write a program that answers this question, write down your choices, and check whether your result is reproducible. Cette fois, le fichier où écrire les réponse sera "answers_banking_llm.txt", sous la forme: Au bout de 50 années, il y a -4396803930182068517325088235103664583272782888960.000000 sur le compte"

For the associativity problem, the ai used float randoms between 0 and 1, and used the approximations, apparently to generate artificially inequality ? That results in more wrong answers: from 92% of right with my program to 82% with the ai's.
For the banking problem, my results and ai's results are the same.
